# ABOVE ATLAS — 12-Week Curriculum
### Two tracks, one spine

---

## THE ARCHITECTURE

Both tracks run the same four-phase spine. Same week numbers, same
psychological sequence, different application. You build one structure
and populate it twice.

| Phase | Weeks | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1. You're the Problem** | 1–3 | See the identity you're actually running |
| **2. Pick a Side** | 4–6 | Decide what you believe and earn the right to say it |
| **3. Open Your Mouth** | 7–9 | Move it from your head into the room |
| **4. Don't Go Back** | 10–12 | Hold it under pressure and lock it in |

**Design rule for every week:** the week is not complete when they
understand it. It's complete when they've done a rep and turned in
proof. Identity doesn't shift from insight. It shifts from evidence
you were someone new and survived it.

# TRACK A — ABOVE ATLAS FOR DOCTORS
*Practicing DCs, 1–20+ years out*

---

## PHASE 1 — YOU'RE THE PROBLEM

### Week 1 — The Subluxation Above Atlas
**The pain:** He's blamed the town, the economy, the insurance
climate, the CA who quit, the patients who "just want to crack and
go." He has never seriously considered that the ceiling is him.

**The shift:** From *my market is the problem* to *my ceiling is my
certainty.*

**The rep:** Record his own report of findings. Audio is enough.
Watch it back and count three things — how many times he hedged, how
long he waited after saying the number, and whether he apologized for
anything. Turn in the count, not the recording.

**Why it opens the program:** Nothing else lands until he's seen it.
Most DCs have never once watched themselves work.

---

### Week 2 — Where You Learned to Shrink
**The pain:** Somewhere between "you're going to be a *real* doctor,
right?" at a family dinner and the first patient who said "so you're
not a medical doctor," he learned to make himself smaller. He
over-explains to MDs. He downplays what he does at parties. He calls
himself a chiropractor apologetically.

**The shift:** From *borrowed legitimacy* to *earned authority.*

**The rep:** Write the origin story of the flinch. When did he first
learn that being a chiropractor required a defense? Who taught him?
Then write what he'd say now.

---

### Week 3 — The Congruence Audit
**The pain:** He recommends 3x/week for 12 weeks and hasn't been
adjusted himself in five months. He tells patients about sleep,
stress, and movement while running on four hours and gas station
coffee. That gap is not hidden. It leaks out as hesitation in the ROF,
and the patient feels it even if nobody names it.

**The shift:** From *selling care* to *transmitting a life he's
actually living.*

**The rep:** Get on a care plan. With a real DC, at full fee, paid.
Book it this week and turn in the receipt. Non-negotiable.

**This week is the sleeper.** It's the one that produces the most
change and the most resistance.

---

## PHASE 2 — PICK A SIDE

### Week 4 — What You Actually Believe
**The pain:** He's philosophically homeless. Too evidence-minded for
the straight crowd, too principled for the mechanical crowd, so he
says nothing and stands nowhere. A patient asks what he believes about
health and he gives a hedge that satisfies no one, least of all him.

**The shift:** From *avoiding the tribes* to *owning a position he can
defend in either room.*

**The deliverable:** A one-page written philosophy. Not a mission
statement — what he believes about the body, healing, and his role.
Read it out loud on the call.

---

### Week 5 — Certainty Without Overclaiming
**The pain:** Two failure modes, and he oscillates. Overclaim — cure
language, curing-everything posts — and he feels like a fraud in
private. Underclaim, hedge everything into "may help with discomfort,"
and he sounds like a PT with worse equipment. Neither one is certainty.

**The shift:** From *volume as confidence* to *precision as
confidence.*

**The deliverable:** His defensible claims list. Every claim he makes
clinically, with what actually backs it, and the exact wording he uses.
The certainty comes from knowing he can defend every sentence, not from
saying it louder.

---

### Week 6 — The Right to Recommend
**The pain:** The profession's original wound. He was trained by
somebody, somewhere, to feel dirty about recommending care. So he
soft-pedals the plan, offers the smaller option first, discounts before
being asked, and tells himself he's being ethical. He's being scared.

**The shift:** From *recommending is selling* to *withholding the
recommendation is the harm.*

**The rep:** Recommend the full plan, at full fee, to the next five
patients. No pre-discount, no "we could start with." Log what happened
with all five, including the ones who said no.

---

## PHASE 3 — OPEN YOUR MOUTH

### Week 7 — The Room
**The pain:** He talks too fast. He fills silence because silence
feels like rejection. He explains the same finding three ways because
he's not sure the first one landed. All of it reads as uncertainty
regardless of the content.

**The shift:** From *filling space* to *holding it.*

**The rep:** Say the number, then say nothing. Count to five in his
head before speaking again. Five patients. Report what happened in the
silence.

---

### Week 8 — The Report of Findings as Leadership
**The pain:** He's presenting information and hoping the patient
draws the conclusion. That's not a report of findings, that's a
briefing. The patient is waiting to be led and he's waiting to be
chosen.

**The shift:** From *presenting options* to *making a recommendation.*

**The build:** Rebuild his Day 1 / Day 2 architecture around a single
question: at what moment does the patient understand this is a
recommendation and not a menu?

*(Your existing Week 6 content lives here.)*

---

### Week 9 — Holding the Line
**The pain:** "I need to talk to my spouse." "That's a lot of money."
"I'll just come in when it hurts." He has heard all three a thousand
times and still gets ambushed by them, because he takes them as
verdicts on his worth instead of normal moments in a conversation.

**The shift:** From *objection as rejection* to *objection as the
patient asking to be led one more time.*

**The rep:** Live role-play on the call, unscripted, with him in the
chair. This is the week that separates the program from a course.

---

## PHASE 4 — DON'T GO BACK

### Week 10 — The Practice Is Downstream
**The pain:** His case average and PVA are attached to his self-worth,
so he either avoids his numbers or lets them run his mood. Both make
him worse in the room.

**The shift:** From *numbers as a verdict on him* to *numbers as a
readout of his certainty.*

**The frame:** Low PVA is not a retention problem, it's a Day 1
problem. Low case average is not a pricing problem, it's a conviction
problem. The numbers point back upstream every time.

*(Your existing Week 5 business content lives here, reframed.)*

---

### Week 11 — Leading People Who Aren't Patients
**The pain:** He has kept a bad hire for eleven months because he
can't have the conversation. His CA runs the front desk on her own
preferences. He's never told an associate the truth about their
performance. He's a doctor in the adjusting room and a conflict-avoidant
employee in his own business.

**The shift:** From *boss who hopes* to *leader who is clear.*

**The rep:** The conversation he's been avoiding. Have it this week.

*(Your existing Week 8 contracts and ownership content lives here.)*

---

### Week 12 — The Doctor You'll Be in Ten Years
**The pain:** Twelve weeks of change with no structure to hold it
reverts inside a quarter. He's done seminars before. He knows the
Monday-after feeling and the six-weeks-later feeling.

**The shift:** From *a program he did* to *standards he keeps.*

**The deliverable:** A written doctor's code. His non-negotiables, his
fee, his plan, his own care, his standards for who he lets in the
practice. Plus a named person who is allowed to check him — because the
subluxation above atlas comes back, and nobody adjusts themselves.

# TRACK B — ABOVE ATLAS FOR STUDENTS
*Chiropractic students, any trimester*

---

## PHASE 1 — YOU'RE THE PROBLEM

### Week 1 — Nobody Is Coming to Give You Permission
**The pain:** Sixteen straight years of being graded. He has been
trained that someone else decides when he's ready, and he's waiting
for that person to appear. They don't appear at graduation. The license
arrives and the feeling doesn't change.

**The shift:** From *waiting to be certified as ready* to *deciding.*

**The rep:** Introduce himself as a doctor to five people this week.
Not "I'm a student." Watch what his body does when he says it. Write
that down.

---

### Week 2 — Where You Learned to Hedge
**The pain:** "I think." "Hopefully." "We can try." "It might help."
Every sentence has an escape hatch, because in school being wrong out
loud costs him. That habit does not disappear when the diploma prints.

**The shift:** From *protecting himself from being wrong* to *being
willing to be certain in front of people.*

**The rep:** Record himself explaining a finding to a classmate. Count
the hedges. Do it again with zero.

---

### Week 3 — The Congruence Audit
**The pain:** He's about to spend a career telling people to get
adjusted, sleep, move, and manage stress, and he's currently running on
energy drinks and hasn't been checked since the last technique lab.

**The shift:** From *studying the principle* to *living under it.*

**The rep:** Get on a care plan with a DC in town. Pay for it out of
his own money. This is also his first real exposure to what a patient
actually experiences on the other side of the table.

---

## PHASE 2 — PICK A SIDE

### Week 4 — What You Actually Believe
**The pain:** Campus tribalism handed him two boxes and told him to
pick. He picked whichever one his favorite instructor was in, and he
can't defend it in either direction without getting emotional.

**The shift:** From *inherited position* to *examined position.*

**The deliverable:** One page. What he believes and why. Read aloud.

---

### Week 5 — Say It at Thanksgiving
**The pain:** His uncle asks what chiropractic actually does. He
either gets defensive, gets technical, or shrugs. Three years and six
figures of debt in, and he cannot explain his own profession to the
people who love him.

**The shift:** From *defending chiropractic* to *explaining it plainly
to anyone.*

**The rep:** Explain what he does, in under sixty seconds, no jargon,
to someone with zero background. Three different people. Refine it
each time.

---

### Week 6 — Your Hands
**The pain:** Technique anxiety. He's scared he'll hurt someone,
scared he'll miss, scared to be watched. He compensates by studying
more theory, because reading is safe and adjusting is not.

**The shift:** From *knowing about the adjustment* to *being someone
who delivers it.*

**The rep:** Reps under observation. Volume, with feedback, in front of
people. There's no book version of this week.

---

## PHASE 3 — OPEN YOUR MOUTH

### Week 7 — The Room
**The pain:** Eye contact, pace, posture, what to do with his hands.
Nobody has ever coached him on how to be in a room with a person who is
scared and in pain. He's been graded on knowledge and never on presence.

**The shift:** From *knowing the answer* to *being the calm in the room.*

---

### Week 8 — Learn the Consult Before You Need It
**The pain:** He will learn the consultation and report of findings by
failing at them on real patients in his first associate job, in front
of a boss who is judging his numbers. That's the default path and it's
a brutal way to learn.

**The shift:** From *figuring it out on the job* to *walking in already
able to do it.*

**The build:** Full consult and ROF architecture, rehearsed until it's
his.

---

### Week 9 — Holding the Line
**The pain:** In clinic he begs. He needs the numbers to graduate, so
he discounts, over-accommodates, and lets patients set the terms. He is
learning, at the exact wrong moment, that his care is worth whatever
someone will tolerate paying.

**The shift:** From *needing the patient* to *serving the patient.*

**The rep:** Live objection role-play. Same as the doctor track, no
easier.

---

## PHASE 4 — DON'T GO BACK

### Week 10 — The Money Reality
**The pain:** Six figures of debt and no idea what a practice grosses,
what overhead runs, what a fair associate contract looks like, or what
a percentage-of-collections deal actually pays. He will sign the first
offer he gets because he's scared, and he'll live inside that decision
for three years.

**The shift:** From *taking what's offered* to *evaluating it.*

**The deliverable:** Read a real associate contract. Line by line.
Know what every clause does.

---

### Week 11 — Choosing Your First Five Years
**The pain:** He thinks the big decision is associate versus start
your own. It isn't. The decision is who he'll be standing next to every
day for five years, because he will become that person's version of a
chiropractor whether he intends to or not.

**The shift:** From *finding a job* to *choosing an environment.*

**The deliverable:** His criteria, written before he interviews
anywhere.

---

### Week 12 — Day One
**The pain:** Most new grads spend the first eighteen months
apologizing for their inexperience, and patients believe them.

**The shift:** From *new doctor* to *doctor.*

**The deliverable:** His written code, his standards, his fee floor,
and the person who's allowed to check him.

## BUILD NOTES

**What survives from the 8-week.** Weeks 2, 3, 5, 6, and 8 of the
current curriculum all carry forward — neurology, scripts, business,
care plans, contracts. They move to Weeks 5, 8, 10, and 11 and get
reframed as proof rather than instruction. You are not writing twelve
weeks from scratch. You're writing about five.

**The four weeks that are genuinely new** are 1, 2, 3, and 6 on the
doctor track. That's the first phase and the sales wound, and
it's the entire reason the program is called Above Atlas instead of
NeuroChiro Mastermind. If you shortcut those four, you've renamed the
8-week and added a month.

**Weeks 3 and 9 are the program.** Congruence and live objection reps
under pressure. Everything else is scaffolding around those two.
Protect the call time for them.

**Live call structure that makes this work:**
- 15 min — reps reported from last week, out loud, no hiding
- 30 min — the week's teaching
- 30 min — live coaching in the chair, one person at a time
- 15 min — this week's rep assigned, specific and dated

**A caution worth taking seriously.** Twelve weeks is 50% more
delivery than your 8-week, three or four times a year, on top of
treating full-time as the only DC in your clinic. Decide before you
build whether those four extra weeks are new content or breathing
room. If it's new content, that's roughly 20 hours of build plus 12
extra live calls per cohort.

**On the pain points above.** These come from what's well documented
about this profession and from what you've told me about your own
practice. You've sat in rooms I haven't. Cut anything that doesn't
ring true and tell me what's missing — the ones you'd add from your
own first three years are probably the sharpest ones in the program.
